Clinical complexity, made obvious

The brief usually shows up as regulation, clinical nuance and a workflow that's been around for twenty years. What goes out is a set of flows, prototypes and UI that a clinician can use on day one. Most of my job is the translating in between.

On a healthcare website or web app, a confusing screen is more than a small design flaw. It can mean a clinician hesitating at exactly the wrong moment, so clarity is really what I'm delivering.

The challenge

The brief that usually ships as a manual

Clinical websites and web apps come with more constraints than almost anything else on the web. Regulations decide what has to appear and when. In clinical work, the edge case is often the main case. And most of it sits on top of old workflows that people have spent years working around.

Left alone, you end up with a dense, powerful tool that needs a training session before anyone can use it. My job is to soak up all that complexity in the design, so the person using it never has to feel it.

The approach

From requirement to something builders can trust

  1. Discover

    Spend time with stakeholders and users until the real brief comes out. Usually it's the constraint nobody wrote down but everyone works around.

  2. Map

    Draw the flow, the journey and every edge case, until the whole team can finally see the same problem in one picture.

  3. Prototype

    Move quickly from wireframes to an interactive prototype, and test the risky paths before anyone writes production code.

  4. Validate

    Test against real clinical scenarios, not just the happy path, and feed whatever breaks back into the design.

  5. Ship

    Hand over UI that's ready to build, with the states, specs and accessibility notes engineers need to build it exactly as designed.

I take on the complexity in the design, so the person using it doesn't have to.
